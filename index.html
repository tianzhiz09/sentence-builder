Learn: the three sentence types

Simple

One independent clause.
Maria studies English.

Compound

Two independent clauses joined by a coordinating conjunction (for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so).
Maria studies English, and she practices every day.

Complex

One independent clause + one dependent clause joined by a subordinating conjunction (because, although, when, if, before, after, while).
Because Maria studies every day, she is improving.

An independent clause has a subject and a verb and can stand alone (“I finished my homework.”). A dependent clause has a subject and a verb but cannot stand alone (“because I was tired”). Connectors such as and, but, so, because, although, when, if, while, after, before show how ideas relate — syntax is connected to meaning, not only correctness.
